    Maxiphobic Min-Heap Algorithms

    Maxiphobic Heap

    A Maxiphobic Heap (introduced by Chris Okasaki) is a mergeable min-heap designed to keep merge paths logarithmic without strict leftist balance constraints—it avoids merging into the "heaviest" subtree.

    Operations

    • Minimum: O(1) — Always at the root.
    • Insert: O(log n) — Creates a 1-node heap and merges it with the existing heap.
    • Delete Min: O(log n) — Removes the root and merges its left and right subtrees.
    • Merge(H1, H2): O(log n)
      1. Compare the two roots; the smaller key stays as the new root.
      2. Take the three remaining subtrees: H1.left, H1.right, and H2 (empty subtrees are highlighted as ∅ with weight 0).
      3. Maxiphobic Rule: The subtree with the largest weight (most nodes) becomes the Left Child directly.
      4. The two smaller subtrees are recursively merged to form the Right Child.

    Build Algorithms

    • Build Random (Sequential): O(n log n) — Inserts elements one by one.
    • Build Bottom-Up (Pairwise): O(n) — Places single-node heaps in a queue, repeatedly merges the front two, and enqueues the result.

    Tips & Keyboard Shortcuts

    • Batch Insert: Enter comma-separated numbers (e.g., 40, 15, 30, 5, 22).
    • Mouse / Touch: Drag to pan, scroll wheel or pinch to zoom, click/tap a node to copy its value, hover to highlight its subtree and inspect weights and depth.
    • Shortcuts: Enter Insert, Space Pause/Resume, ←/→ Step Back/Forward, Ctrl+Z Undo, Esc Close Help.
